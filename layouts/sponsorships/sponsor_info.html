  • Advance Reproducible Science. Support integration of Galaxy workflows with Bioconductor packages to enable transparent, end-to-end analyses reproducible across labs.

  • Empower Data-Intensive Research. Fuel sessions on single-cell, imaging, and multi-omics, showcasing high-throughput methods accelerating discoveries in genomics, ecology, and clinical research.
  • Bridge Communities. Unite experimental biologists, computational scientists, and software engineers to foster cross-disciplinary innovation and drive open-source ecosystems forward.

Silver Sponsors

Chan Zuckerberg Initiative

The Chan Zuckerberg Initiative (CZI) funds a portfolio of open-source software programs aimed at maintaining and advancing critical bioinformatics tools across biomedical research. Since 2016, CZI’s Essential Open Source Software for Science program has awarded over $11 million in grants to projects that demonstrate significant impact and a sustainable community around tools such as the Common Workflow Language and BioImage Analysis frameworks. In collaboration with the Kavli Foundation and the Wellcome Trust, CZI supports rapid development of plugins for interactive visualization platforms like napari, enhancing reproducibility and accessibility of image analysis workflows. CZI also underpins major consortia efforts such as the Human Cell Atlas, providing funding and technical support for constructing open-reference maps of healthy human cells with interoperable data standards. Their CellxGene Discover portal offers an intuitive interface for exploring multimodal single-cell datasets, fostering community-driven annotation and hypothesis generation in spatial transcriptomics and beyond.

Bronze Sponsors

Limagrain

Limagrain leads global agricultural innovation through advanced plant genetics and breeding. Their work in data-driven selection pipelines accelerates development of resilient crop varieties.

Founded in 1965 as a cooperative of French farmers, Limagrain has grown to become a world leader in seed production and plant breeding, operating across five continents. Their research teams employ high-throughput genotyping and phenotyping platforms, integrating environmental data to predict trait performance under climate stress. Limagrain collaborates with academic and public institutions to share open data and methodologies, fostering transparency and reproducibility in agricultural genomics. They have developed proprietary pipelines that combine Galaxy’s workflow management with custom Bioconductor packages for genome-wide association studies. Limagrain’s commitment to sustainable agriculture is reflected in their projects on nitrogen-use efficiency and disease resistance, addressing global food security challenges.

GalaxyWorks

GalaxyWorks specializes in deploying and optimizing Galaxy platforms for large-scale analyses. Their expertise ensures robust, scalable infrastructures for high-performance bioinformatics.

Founded by veteran systems administrators and bioinformaticians, GalaxyWorks offers turnkey Galaxy solutions tailored to institutional needs, from on-premises clusters to multi-cloud architectures. They contribute upstream to Galaxy’s codebase, developing features like federated data access, workflow dashboards, and role-based access control. Their professional services include performance tuning for petabyte-scale datasets, enabling genomics consortia to process terabytes per week with minimal downtime. GalaxyWorks also supports hybrid workflows that combine Galaxy with Kubernetes-managed microservices for interactive analysis. By integrating real-time monitoring and automated scaling, their deployments maintain consistent performance even under variable workloads. They regularly publish whitepapers on Galaxy best practices and lead community training workshops, strengthening the open-source ecosystem.

R Consortium

The R Consortium supports open-source development for statistical computing. They fund projects and events that strengthen the R ecosystem for data science.

Established in 2015 under the Linux Foundation, the R Consortium has distributed over $2 million in grants to support package development, interoperability standards, and community events. They oversee working groups focused on R in the enterprise, bioinformatics interoperability, and package checks automation. The consortium sponsors R-Hub, a continuous integration service that validates R packages across platforms, ensuring reliability for end-users. They also maintain the Bioconductor Infrastructure Enhancement project, funding improvements to package build systems and Docker-based deployment. The R Consortium organizes global R user groups and conferences, fostering knowledge exchange and mentoring opportunities.

Friends of GBCC

GigaScience

GigaScience pioneers open-access publishing with interactive data objects. Their platform links articles to datasets and workflows, championing transparency in life sciences.

Since its launch in 2012, GigaScience has published over 500 data-driven studies, covering genomics, metagenomics, and systems biology. They maintain a unique repository that integrates data, code, and computational environments, enabling direct reuse of research outputs. GigaScience collaborates with INRA and other research institutes to curate high-quality datasets, promoting standardization and interoperability. Their editorial board includes experts in bioinformatics, software development, and data curation, ensuring rigorous evaluation of both science and data. The journal supports community-developed tools like Galaxy workflows and Bioconductor packages, providing direct links from articles to executable workflows. GigaScience also hosts training events on data citation and reproducible research practices, empowering authors to meet evolving open-science mandates.

Other Partners

National Human Genome Research Institute

The National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI) is the U.S. federal institute dedicated to advancing genomic research to improve human health. Established in 1989 to lead NIH’s role in the International Human Genome Project, NHGRI oversaw the completion of the first human reference genome in 2003 and has since expanded its mission to understanding the structure, function, and variation of the genome in health and disease. Through its intramural and extramural programs, NHGRI funds cutting-edge projects in genome sequencing, gene editing, and computational genomics, developing resources such as the Genome Reference Consortium and the All of Us Research Program to democratize access to genomic data and technologies.

NHGRI’s strategic vision now emphasizes the translation of genomic discoveries into clinical practice, supporting initiatives in precision medicine, ethical, legal, and social implications (ELSI) research, and workforce development. By fostering collaborations with academic institutions, non-profits, and industry partners, NHGRI drives innovation in areas such as single-cell genomics, synthetic biology, and long-read sequencing, ensuring that genomic tools remain interoperable, reproducible, and accessible to diverse research communities.
