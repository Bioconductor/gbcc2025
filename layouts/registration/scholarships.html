Scholarships

Discover two unique scholarship opportunities offered by the JXTX Foundation and the Galaxy Project. Whether you’re interested in the JXTX Scholarships or the JJ Travel Fellowships, there’s an opportunity designed to support emerging talent in genomics, data sciences, and computational biology.

Scholarship Opportunities at GBCC 2025

We’re proud to present two separate scholarships:

  • JXTX Scholarships: Awarded in two tiers – one including transportation support, and one for registration only.
    Apply online using the button below.
  • JJ Travel Fellowships: Designed to sponsor graduate students or postdoctoral researchers.
    Simply apply by emailing the requested documents.

JXTX Scholarships

JXTX Scholarships will be awarded in two tiers:

  • First tier: Two individuals receive conference registration plus transportation (up to US$1,000).
  • Second tier: Two individuals receive conference registration only.
Apply for JXTX Scholarship

The JJ Travel Fellowships

These fellowships sponsor up to two graduate students or postdoctoral researchers to attend the Galaxy and Bioconductor Community Conference (GBCC 2025).

Key Dates

  • Deadline for applications: April 8, 2025
  • Winners announced: April 17, 2025

Applicants should be prepared to present their work at the conference – either as a talk or a poster.

Application Process

To apply for the JJ Travel Fellowship, please prepare the following documents and email them to jj-fund@jxtxfoundation.org:

  • A one-page write-up detailing:
    • a) Your scientific interests
    • b) Your interest in the Galaxy platform
    • c) Reasons for attending GBCC 2025 (include the conference abstract title)
  • Your curriculum vitae (CV)
  • A reference letter from your advisor

Our Mission

The JJ Travel Fellowships are designed to empower emerging researchers and broaden participation in the Galaxy ecosystem. By supporting future innovators, these awards honor JJ’s legacy as a mentor and trailblazer in the field.

About JJ: James Johnson (JJ)

James Johnson, or JJ as he was affectionately known, was a senior software developer at the Minnesota Supercomputing Institute at the University of Minnesota. As one of the early adopters and contributors to the Galaxy project, his work has enabled countless research initiatives and inspired many young scientists.

Support the JJ Travel Fellowships

Your support helps ensure that financial constraints do not hinder talented researchers. Donations play a crucial role in sustaining the JJ Travel Fellowships and advancing scientific innovation.
